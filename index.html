Methodology & data notes

S-curve (logistic) model

For flow metrics (quarterly revenue, orders booked), fits a 3-parameter logistic K / (1 + e^-r(t-t0)) to the cumulative series via grid search over the asymptote K plus OLS on the logit-linearized transform, refined with nonlinear least squares. The inflection point t0 marks the quarter where the underlying flow itself is forecast to peak — after which cumulative additions keep rising but at a decelerating pace. For stock metrics (backlog, RPO), the logistic is fit directly to the level; t0 marks peak growth-rate of the stock, not its absolute peak.

Local regression model

A recency-weighted quadratic (half-life 4 quarters) fit to the most recent 8 quarters of the level series. Solves for the vertex where the fitted derivative is zero. If the curve is still convex (accelerating) over the trailing window, no local peak is reported — that is itself informative: it means recent prints show no deceleration yet.

Model vs. actual divergence

Each S-curve card reports how far the latest actual quarter ran above or below what the fitted curve implied. A large positive gap means real activity is compounding faster than a smooth logistic would predict — the more classic overheating signal than a specific "peak date."

Known limitations

A single logistic assumes one smooth growth wave. Metrics with strong quarter-to-quarter seasonality (Eaton, GE Vernova, and Alphabet's total revenue) fit the cumulative trend well but fit the quarterly flow shape only loosely — flagged as "fair" or "weak" fit quality in each chart. Eaton discloses orders only as segment-level rolling 12-month growth %, not a dollar figure, and total backlog $ only in scattered quarters — both are shown as-disclosed rather than estimated.
